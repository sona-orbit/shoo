Hide Windows and Desktop Files Before Sharing Your Mac Screen

The safest order before sharing your screen: turn off notifications, share a single window, hide other apps with a shortcut, then tuck away your desktop files. macOS can do all of this, but it takes many steps. If you have meetings every day, you can tie it all to one sticker. One click hides everything. Another click brings it all back.

1. Turn off notifications while sharing

The biggest risk is not an open window. It's a message notification popping up halfway through your share. In System Settings › Notifications, there is an option for allowing notifications when mirroring or sharing the display. Turn it off, and notifications are paused while you share.

You only set this once. It works every time you share your screen from then on, so do it first.

2. Share one window, not the whole screen

Most video meeting apps let you choose “Entire Screen” or “Single Window” when you share. If you pick a single window, nothing else can be seen.

The downside: if you need to switch to another file midway, you have to stop sharing and pick again. To save time, many people share the whole screen. Then everyone can see whatever is open behind your slides.

3. Hide other apps with a shortcut

If you do need to share the whole screen, hide the apps you don't want seen before you start. These are the shortcuts Apple lists:

ShortcutWhat it does
Command-HHides all windows of the frontmost app
Option-Command-HKeeps only the frontmost app and hides all other apps
Command-MMinimizes the frontmost window to the Dock
Command-Mission Control or Fn-F11Shows the desktop (windows move aside for a moment)

Option-Command-H is the fastest. Switch to the presentation you're giving, press it once, and every other app hides. But after the meeting, you have to click each app back one by one. You also have to rearrange your windows yourself.

4. Tuck away your desktop files

Once the windows are hidden, what shows is often a desktop full of screenshots and files named “final_v3”. In System Settings › Desktop & Dock, you can turn off Show Items on Desktop. The files stop showing on the desktop. They are still there, just out of sight.

Remember to turn it back on after the meeting. Otherwise you may think your files are gone.

5. Doing it every time? Tie it to one sticker

All four steps work. But you have to run through them before every meeting, then undo them one by one. I forget too, so I made Shoo! Stickers:

  • Tear a sticker from the sticker book, put it on your desktop, and pick which apps or windows it looks after.
  • Click the sticker before a meeting and those windows all hide. Click it again and everything returns to where it was.
  • Turn on “Hide desktop items too” to cover the files on your desktop with your own wallpaper.
  • Safari, Chrome and Notes can hide a single window. Other apps hide as a whole.

For example, make a “Screen Share” sticker that handles chat, shopping, music and desktop files. Then make a “Someone walks by” sticker that hides only your private things.

7. FAQ

Do hidden windows get closed?

No. Command-H and Shoo both only hide. The app keeps running and nothing is lost.

If I share a single window, can people still see notifications?

Usually not, because people only see that one window. But if you share the whole screen, turn off notifications while sharing in System Settings › Notifications.

Why can Shoo only hide some apps as a whole?

Right now, only Safari, Chrome and Notes let Shoo hide a single window. Other apps are hidden as a whole.
